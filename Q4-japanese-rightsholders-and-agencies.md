Compiled 2026-09-09. ~400 sources retrieved to date. **Japan only**, per client scope.
All figures JPY. Latest available fiscal year unless noted.

**This replaces an earlier version** that was both too thin and wrongly included Chinese

## 0. THE TWO FINDINGS THAT MATTER MOST

### Finding 1 — Rights income is the strategic growth engine at every Japanese publisher, and it just crossed a threshold

**Shueisha's 事業収入 (rights and merchandise income) exceeded JPY 110 bn in FY2026/5 and
surpassed its publishing sales for the first time in the company's history.**

That is not a detail about one publisher. Across the four largest Japanese content owners the
same line is the growth driver:

| Company | Rights/licensing line | Value | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shueisha** | 事業収入 (rights + merch) | **>JPY 110 bn** | **surpassed publishing sales for the first time** |
| **Toei Animation** | 版権事業 segment | **JPY 48.905 bn** | from 50.582 bn (−3.3%) |
| **Kodansha** | ライツ収入 | **JPY 28.540 bn** | −11.8% |
| **Shogakukan** | 版権収入等 | **JPY 15.729 bn** (87th term) | **+26.8%** |
| **COVER Corp** | Licensing / Tie-up | **JPY 7.198 bn** | **+25.3%** |

**Implication for an entrant:** these companies are structurally motivated to sign more
licensing deals, and several of them say so. Shogakukan's +26.8% was explicitly attributed to
film and TV adaptations of its manga. COVER attributes its +25.3% to growth in **deal count and
number of counterparty companies**. The door is being pushed open from the inside.

### Finding 2 — But FY2026 was a *down* year for licensing at several of them, and that matters for timing

| Company | Licensing direction | Cause given |
|---|---|---|
| **KADOKAWA** | **licensing income −21.1%**; anime/live-action segment swung to an **operating loss** | reaction to the prior year's 【推しの子】 distribution and licensing income |
| **Kodansha** | rights income **−11.8%** | not stated |
| **Toei Animation** | 版権 segment down ~3.3% | but full-year guidance was **revised UP** on strong **overseas** licensing |
| **Tsuburaya Productions** | revenue **−15.4%**, operating profit **−67.0%** | overseas decline; **China licensing fell** as block-toy and trading-card partners revised ranges |
| **COVER Corp** | licensing **+25.3%** but **net income −45.7%** | streaming/EC adjustment; **North American tariff impact** |

**Read:** licensing revenue at Japanese IP holders is highly cyclical and hit-dependent. A
counterparty coming off a down year is more receptive to new categories, and several of these are.
Conversely COVER, growing licensing but losing profit, is explicitly moving to a **"disciplined
growth phase"** prioritising capital efficiency — which usually means harder terms, not softer.

## 2. LICENSING OBSERVABILITY — who discloses what, and why it matters

This is the practical answer to "can I benchmark a deal before signing one?" Ranked by how much
the counterparty reveals:

| Tier | Company | What is visible |
|---|---|---|
| **Best** | **Toei Animation** | A dedicated **版権事業 segment** at JPY 48.905 bn — roughly **52% of company revenue**. Full segment P&L in the tanshin |
| | **Kodansha** | Revenue split into five named lines including **ライツ収入 JPY 28.540 bn** |
| | **Shogakukan** | **版権収入等 JPY 15.729 bn** disclosed, with the driver named (film/TV adaptations) |
| | **COVER Corp** | **Licensing/Tie-up JPY 7.198 bn** — the only VTuber agency to split it out |
| **Partial** | **Shueisha** | 事業収入 >JPY 110 bn, but rights and merchandise are **combined** |
| | **Sanrio** | "Merchandise sales and licensing" = **90.3% of revenue** — combined, not split |
| | **KADOKAWA** | Licensing movement given as a **percentage** (−21.1%), not an absolute |
| **None** | **ANYCOLOR** | Goods buried in **commerce (~70% of revenue)**; **no licensing line at all** |
| | **Adways** | 774 inc. sits inside "Other" with influencer marketing and **sauna operations** |
| | **Yostar, Khara/Groundworks, Bunka Broadcasting, Universal Music Japan** | Private, revenue undisclosed |

**Consequence:** an entrant can build a credible view of licensing economics from Toei Animation,
Kodansha, Shogakukan and COVER. It cannot from ANYCOLOR, Yostar or Khara. That should shape
which counterparty you approach first — **approach the ones whose numbers you can read.**

**And still: no royalty rate appears anywhere in ~400 sources** (`gaps.csv` G001). Every one of
these disclosures is an aggregate. The rate is obtainable only through direct contact.

## 3. COMPANY PROFILES

### 3.1 COVER Corp — hololive — TSE 5253

**FY2026/3 (non-consolidated):** revenue **49.33 bn** (+13.7%) · operating profit **~7.0 bn** ·
net income **3.016 bn (−45.7%)**.
Segments: Merchandising **23.747 bn (+15.6%)** · Live/Events **9.247 bn (+18.7%)** ·
**Licensing/Tie-up 7.198 bn (+25.3%)** · residual streaming and own EC ~9.1 bn, declining.
**DERIVED:** operating margin **14.2%** · revenue per talent **JPY 632 mn**.

**People:** **78 talents** (June 2026). Average annual salary **JPY 6.10 mn**. Headcount rose
**+142 staff** year-on-year on expanded hiring — exact total is behind a paywall (`gaps.csv` G062).

**Structure:** runs hololive (female) and **holostars** (male; JP 12 + EN 10 as of April 2026).
**hololive Indonesia, English and DEV_IS were integrated into hololive on 2026-09-07** — two days
before this compilation, and material because **all four AVIOT unit SKUs are hololive English**.

**Licensing posture:** the only rightsholder in the dataset with a visible licensing line, and it
credits growth to more **trading agents**, more **deals**, and more **counterparty companies**.
Merchandising growth is driven by the **hololive OFFICIAL CARD GAME**, not electronics — do not
read merchandising growth as collab-hardware demand.

**Risk flag:** revenue +13.7% against net income −45.7%, with **North American tariff impact**
named as a cause, and an explicit shift to a **"disciplined growth phase"** on profit quality and
capital efficiency. A licensor in that position hardens terms.

### 3.2 ANYCOLOR — Nijisanji — TSE 5032

**FY2026/4:** revenue **55.681 bn (+29.9%)** · operating profit **20.172 bn (+23.9%)** ·
ordinary **20.197 bn** · net **14.091 bn (+22.4%)**. Commerce ~**70%** of revenue.
Q1 absolutes: commerce **10.394 bn**, events **2.117 bn**.
**DERIVED:** operating margin **36.2%** · revenue per talent **~JPY 371 mn** ·
**revenue per employee JPY 104.7 mn**.

**People: 532 employees (non-consolidated) plus 34 temporary.** Average annual salary
**JPY 4.96 mn**. **Average tenure 2.3 years; average age 31.8.** ~**150 domestic livers**
(~170–180 with NIJISANJI EN); 8 new debuts on 2026-04-19.

**Model, as the company frames it:** acquire and retain fans via live streaming, then monetise
through **commerce** (goods) and **promotion** (corporate advertising). Live streaming stays
membership-centric. FY2026/4 Q4 drivers were the **Nijisanji 8th anniversary** goods programme,
advance sales for **Nijisanji Fes 2026**, and CD/Blu-ray.

**FY2027/4 guidance: revenue 56–60 bn but profit DOWN** on personnel cost.

**The comparison that matters:**

| | ANYCOLOR | COVER |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | **55.681 bn** | 49.33 bn |
| Growth | **+29.9%** | +13.7% |
| Operating margin | **36.2%** | 14.2% |
| Net income trend | **+22.4%** | **−45.7%** |
| Employees | **532** | not disclosed (+142 YoY) |
| Avg salary | 4.96 mn | **6.10 mn** |
| Talents | ~150–180 | 78 |
| Revenue/talent | ~371 mn | **632 mn** |
| Licensing visible | **No** | **Yes** |
| Licensing style | **individuals** — 12+ have own SKUs | **units** — 5 talents per SKU |

COVER extracts more per talent and pays its staff more; ANYCOLOR converts to profit far better on
a bench twice the size. For a licensee: **ANYCOLOR = more counterparties and a proven
per-individual template but zero benchmarkable economics. COVER = fewer, larger unit deals with
readable numbers and tightening terms.**

### 3.3 Sanrio — TSE 8136 — the most profitable licensor in Japan

**FY2026/3:** revenue **194.088 bn (+33.9%)** — record · operating profit **77.859 bn (+50.3%)** ·
ordinary **79.335 bn (+48.4%)** · net **54.608 bn (+30.9%)**.
**DERIVED operating margin 40.1%** — the highest of any company in this dataset.
**Employees 2,157 (+712 YoY).** **DERIVED revenue per employee JPY 90.0 mn.**
**Merchandise sales and licensing = 90.3% of revenue. Japan = 58.5%.**
Fifth consecutive year of record revenue and profit. FY2027/3 Q1 revenue **52.0 bn**, a record
quarter, though profit was held back by rising overseas costs; FY2027 net income forecast **+16.8%**.

Growth attributed to **Hello Kitty**, plus anniversary programmes for **Kuromi** and
**My Melody**, with **Pompompurin's 30th** running from January 2026.

**Relevance:** Sanrio licensed Kuromi, Cinnamoroll, Hello Kitty and Kiki & Lala to **three
separate audio brands** — AVIOT, Zeeny and Hi-Unit. It is the most promiscuous licensor found and
its audio deals sit at the **lowest price points in the market** (AVIOT 18,700–19,800; Zeeny
outlet 5,400). At 40% operating margin and 90% of revenue from licensing and goods, earphones are
one of hundreds of categories and it has no reason to grant favourable terms.

### 3.4 Toei — TSE 9605 — and Toei Animation — TSE 4816

**Toei (parent), FY2026/3:** revenue **185.333 bn (+3.0%)**, operating profit **36.096 bn (+2.7%)**.
Five segments: 映像関連 (visual, the core — film/TV production, distribution and **rights
licensing**), 興行関連 (exhibition, **+33.0%**), 催事関連 (events, **+16.1%**), 観光不動産,
建築内装. **Toei is the rightsholder for Kamen Rider 555, Kamen Rider W and Super Sentai** —
three AVIOT SKUs including **TE-D01v-SSS at JPY 27,500**, and the tokusatsu cluster is the only
partner class with no Chinese equivalent.

**Toei Animation, FY2026/3:** guidance revised **up** — revenue **93.6 bn** (from 88.0 bn, +6.4%),
operating profit **31.0 bn** (from 26.0 bn, **+19.2%**), ordinary **33.4 bn**, net **25.0 bn
(+30.9%)**, year-end dividend raised JPY 41 → 44.
**版権事業 (rights) segment: JPY 48.905 bn, from 50.582 bn** — i.e. **~52% of company revenue is
licensing.** The upward revision was driven by **overseas licensing above plan**, a weak yen, and
securities gains. Toei Animation is the **Digimon** rightsholder (ONKYO ANW-C010).

**Why Toei Animation is the single best benchmark in this project:** it is a listed company where
**licensing is the majority of revenue and is reported as its own segment with a full P&L.** No
other counterparty offers that.

### 3.5 Aniplex — Sony Music subsidiary — the fastest-growing rightsholder

**FY2026/3:** revenue **211.595 bn (+45.1%)** · operating profit **39.083 bn (+62.2%)** ·
ordinary **44.404 bn (+56.4%)** · net **31.058 bn (+46.3%)**.
**FY2025/3:** revenue 145.832 bn (−3.5%), operating 24.098 bn (+3.4%), net 21.235 bn (+6.3%).

A **+45% revenue year with +62% operating profit growth.** Aniplex sits under Sony Music
Entertainment Japan and is classified under **music**, not pictures, which is why Sony Group's
own disclosures rarely surface it. It is the **Madoka Magica** rightsholder (AVIOT TE-V1R-MMM).
Historically it reached ¥206.2 bn in FY2021/3 on the Demon Slayer film.

**Read:** a counterparty with this much momentum has the least need to experiment with small
hardware categories — but also the deepest pockets for co-marketing if it chooses to.

### 3.6 KADOKAWA — TSE 9468 — the receptive one

**FY2026/3:** revenue **282.908 bn (+1.8%)** · operating profit **8.102 bn (−51.3%)** ·
ordinary **11.701 bn (−34.1%)** · net **1.278 bn (−82.7%)**.
Publishing/IP-creation held up, but **licensing income fell 21.1%** and the **anime/live-action
segment swung to an operating loss**, both attributed to the reaction against the prior year's
【推しの子】 distribution and licensing income. Games declined on the ELDEN RING comparison.
Web services returned to profit; education grew.

KADOKAWA holds **KonoSuba, Overlord and Dungeon Meshi** — three confirmed collab franchises across
AVIOT and Universal Music. **It is the largest Japanese IP holder currently having a bad
licensing year**, which makes it the most plausible counterparty for a new category.

### 3.7 The manga publishers — Shueisha, Shogakukan, Kodansha, ShoPro

**Shueisha**, 85th term (Jun 2025 – May 2026): revenue **228.954 bn (−0.1%)**, net income
**19.361 bn (−0.5%)**. **事業収入 exceeded 110 bn and passed publishing sales for the first
time.** The prior term grew revenue +12.2% with rights and merchandise income **+35.6%**.
Rightsholder for **Jujutsu Kaisen, Gintama, Haikyu!!, Oshi no Ko and Prince of Tennis** —
**five confirmed AVIOT franchises, more than any other single company.**

**Shogakukan**, 88th term (Mar 2025 – Feb 2026): revenue **103.567 bn (−5.5%)**, ordinary profit
**2.293 bn (−53.0%)**, net **2.623 bn (−28.0%)**. In the 87th term revenue was 109.616 bn (+0.8%)
with **版権収入等 15.729 bn (+26.8%)**, explicitly driven by film and TV adaptations of its manga.
Rightsholder for **Ranma ½, Frieren and Detective Conan**.

**Kodansha**, 87th term (Dec 2024 – Nov 2025): revenue **169.169 bn (−1.1%)**, operating profit
**10.4 bn (−3.1%)**, ordinary 15.1 bn (+5.5%), net **11.188 bn (+19.4%)**.
Revenue lines: products **43.021 bn (−8.6%)** · **digital 86.191 bn (+8.0%)** ·
**rights income 28.540 bn (−11.8%)** · advertising 6.929 bn (−2.6%) · other 1.282 bn ·
real estate 3.008 bn. Rightsholder for **Go-toubun no Hanayome**.

**小学館集英社プロダクション ShoPro**, FY2026/3: net income **2.415 bn (−9.6%)**. **This is the
joint licensing and merchandising arm of Shogakukan and Shueisha** — for an entrant seeking
either publisher's IP, ShoPro is likely the actual door rather than the publisher itself.

### 3.8 Tsuburaya Productions — the cautionary case

**FY2026/3: revenue 13.87 bn (−15.4%), operating profit 0.93 bn (−67.0%).**
Domestic grew on the **Ultraman 60th anniversary** programme and "strategic collaborations to
raise IP value" — language that directly describes this category. Overseas fell on geopolitical
risk and product-cycle adjustment, with **China licensing income down** as block-toy and
trading-card partners revised their ranges after one-off hits settled.
Parent **Tsuburaya Fields Holdings (TSE Prime 2767)** Q3 cumulative: revenue **154.617 bn (+58.2%)**,
operating profit **18.531 bn (+97.3%)** — driven by pachinko/pachislot, not IP.

**Two lessons.** First, Tsuburaya explicitly credits collaborations with raising IP value, so this
category has an internal advocate there. Second, its overseas licensing collapse shows how fast
a licensor's merchandise partners can churn — relevant to any entrant assuming multi-year terms.

### 3.9 Game and content publishers

**Yostar** — 株式会社Yostar, HQ Chiyoda (Sumitomo Fudosan Akihabara Ekimae Bldg 11F), established
**2017-01-16**, **319 employees (2026-01-01)**, Japanese subsidiary of 上海悠星網絡科技有限公司,
owns 株式会社Yostar Pictures, began in-house development August 2024. **Revenue undisclosed by
company policy.** Blue Archive cumulative worldwide revenue exceeded **USD 240 mn to Jan 2023 with
Japan at 75%**; it is ~30% of Yostar's Japanese revenue and its #2 title.
**Yostar is the counterparty on four confirmed audio collaborations** — AVIOT Blue Archive, AVIOT
Azur Lane waves 1 and 2, and **final's two COTSUBU for ASMR Blue Archive editions.** It is the
only rightsholder appearing across both a mainstream collab operator **and** the ASMR specialist,
which makes it the single most proven receptive counterparty in the dataset.

**COLOPL (TSE 3668)** — FY2026/9 H1: revenue **10.088 bn (−28.2%)**, operating profit
**0.533 bn (−62.3%)**. Entertainment segment 9.82 bn (−18.2%) with operating profit 0.655 bn
(returned to profit); investment segment 0.168 bn (−86.8%), operating loss 0.123 bn.
Shironeko Project standalone revenue is **not disclosed**; a third-party estimate puts monthly
gross at ~JPY 40.77 mn (May 2026). Rightsholder for **Shironeko Project** — two ONKYO SKUs.

**Broccoli** — reference FY2019/2 revenue **5.975 bn (+10.4%)** with **Uta no Prince-sama more
than half of it**; the franchise itself has been valued at **JPY 10–30 bn**. Later periods show
volatility (FY2023/2 Q3 revenue 3.663 bn, −22.5%). Rightsholder for **Uta no Prince-sama** —
three AVIOT SKUs, and notably **sold through Broccoli's own store rather than AVIOT's**, the only
such case found.

**Nitroplus** — founded 2000; FY2023/8 revenue **4.0 bn**, net income **0.623 bn**.
**CyberAgent (TSE 4751) acquired 72.5% on 2024-07-01 for JPY 16.7 bn**, making it a CyberAgent
consolidated subsidiary. Business model is licensing fees on own content plus production-committee
rights. **すーぱーそに子 Super Sonico originated as the image character for the NITRO SUPER SONIC
live event** — Nitroplus is the counterparty on **three Zeeny SKUs**. The ownership change means
the Super Sonico counterparty is now effectively CyberAgent.

### 3.10 Khara / Groundworks — and the rights-subsidiary pattern

**株式会社カラー Khara** — founded **2006-05-17** by **庵野秀明 Hideaki Anno** as an independent
break from Gainax. HQ Suginami, Tokyo. Representative director Anno; vice-representative
尾方隆之 Ogata Tomoyuki; directors include 鶴巻和哉 Tsurumaki Kazuya and 安野モヨコ Anno Moyoco.
Stated business includes **rights ownership** and talent development. **Financials not public.**

**Khara established a dedicated rights-management subsidiary, グラウンドワークス Groundworks.**
So the actual licensing counterparty for Evangelion — **five confirmed SKUs** (final's four
EVA2020 models plus AVIOT TE-V1R-EVA) — is Groundworks, not Khara.

**This is a pattern worth naming.** Several Japanese IP owners route licensing through a dedicated
entity: Khara → Groundworks; Shogakukan and Shueisha → **ShoPro**; Bunka Broadcasting →
**Bunka Broadcast Extend**. **An entrant approaching the creative company is at the wrong door.**

### 3.11 Music labels and media — the routes to seiyuu

**Bandai Namco Holdings (TSE 7832)** — FY2026/3 revenue **1,348.246 bn (+8.6%)**, a record;
operating profit +5%; Q3 cumulative passed **JPY 1 trillion** for the first time. Gundam IP hit
**188.1 bn** in Q3 (+62.5%). The relevant segment is **映像音楽事業 (Visual & Music Production):
revenue 95.506 bn (+5.3%), segment profit 12.181 bn (+3.4%)**. Group strategy explicitly names
**strengthening licensing and live events** and global music expansion via **Bandai Namco Music
Live** and Udo Music Office.

**Kiramune's exact corporate lineage, now resolved:** a record label launched **April 2009** by
**バンダイナムコミュージックライブ** (formerly **Lantis**, then Bandai Namco Live Creative) and
**バンダイナムコフィルムワークス** (formerly **Bandai Visual**). Activities extend beyond records
to **キラフェス Kiramune Music Festival** and **リーディングライブ** reading/drama stage shows.
**20 members in 5 units** as of 2026, including the six-member **IXIS** added 2025-11-16.

**Bandai Namco Music Live** — FY2023/3 net income **4.626 bn (+82%)**.
**Bandai Namco Filmworks** — formed **April 2022** with **Sunrise as the surviving entity**,
absorbing Bandai Namco Arts' visual business and Bandai Namco Rights Marketing. It is the
**core company of the group's IP Produce unit**, and its stated business includes
**copyright and rights management**. Net income: FY2024/3 **3.970 bn (−25.6%)** →
FY2025/3 **5.906 bn (+48.8%)** → FY2026/3 **3.319 bn (−43.8%)**.

**Universal Music Japan** — ユニバーサル ミュージック合同会社, HQ Shibuya, founded **1990-04-20**
as PolyGram K.K., CEO **藤倉尚 Fujikura Naoshi**, **~550 employees**, capital
**JPY 29.502 bn**, **revenue not disclosed**. This is the **TOoKA BASE** operator — a *seller* in
this market, not a licensor, and the only one that owns both the IP relationships and the hardware
brand. Note `gaps.csv` G056: TOoKA BASE appears dormant in the collaboration-earphone category.

**Bunka Broadcasting** — private. Business spans radio production and sales, internet radio,
multimedia and film planning, broadcast technology, and real-estate leasing. Its **A&G**
(Anime & Game) block runs **~30 terrestrial programmes** Friday–Sunday evenings plus
**~100 programmes on the internet channel 超!A&G+**. **文化放送エクステンド Bunka Broadcast
Extend** exists specifically to **monetise radio-born content** and is housed in Bunka Broadcasting
Media Plus. It is the broker named in the Toyo Keizai analysis (CEO 内田裕之 Hiroyuki Uchida), and
its programme **A&G NEXT STEP HOOOOPE! is itself an AVIOT SKU** (TE-V1R-HOP, JPY 19,800).

**Nippon Television Holdings (TSE 9404)** — FY2026/3 revenue **484.4 bn (+4.9%)**, operating profit
**69.3 bn (+26.2%)**, ordinary **82.0 bn (+24.9%)**. Segments: Content/Media, Wellness, Real Estate.
**Merchandise revenue reached 26.275 bn at Q3 (+0.717 bn).** Nippon TV is both an IP source and a

## 4. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR DEAL DESIGN

**Ranked by how approachable each counterparty type looks on the evidence:**

1. **Yostar** — four confirmed audio collabs including two with the ASMR specialist. Most proven
   receptive party. 319 staff, undisclosed revenue, mid-size and focused.
2. **774 inc. and viviON** — small agencies producing the deepest, most design-led collaborations.
   774's parent is only JPY 11.8 bn; viviON owns the ASMR content platform outright.
3. **KADOKAWA** — the largest Japanese IP holder currently in a licensing downturn (−21.1%),
   holding three confirmed franchises. Most likely of the majors to try a new category.
4. **Tsuburaya** — explicitly credits collaborations with raising IP value, coming off a −67%
   operating profit year. Motivated, but its overseas partner churn is a warning.
5. **Toei Animation** — the best *benchmark* counterparty because licensing is a disclosed
   majority-revenue segment, even if the deal itself is standard-form.
6. **Independent talent** — Suou Patra, Shigure Ui, Kagura Mea. Single counterparty, no licence
   stack, and every documented commercial standout in this project.
7. **COVER** — readable economics but tightening terms and a −45.7% net income year.
8. **Sanrio, Aniplex, Bandai Namco** — 40%+ margins, record years, hundreds of categories.
   Standard-form licences, no leverage, minimal creative input.
9. **Aoni, 81 Produce, Mausu, I'm Enterprise** — no precedent at all. Unknown whether closed.

**The consistent pattern, restated with the Japanese-only evidence:** creative depth is inversely
correlated with counterparty size and profitability. Sanrio at 40% operating margin yields
cosmetic reskins at the market's lowest prices. 774 inc., whose parent earns a twentieth of
Sanrio's revenue, yields talent-designed products. Independent individuals yield co-engineered
ones. **If the client's advantage is engineering, the counterparty tier that rewards it is the
small-agency and independent tier — not the mega-IP tier.**
