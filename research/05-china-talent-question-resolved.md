# The China human-talent question — resolved, with evidence of absence

Retrieved 2026-09-07.

---

## THE FINDING

**China monetises voice talent at the platform and IP level, not at the individual-talent level,
and it does not use headphones as the merchandise vehicle.**

This is supported by (a) systematic negative search across individual talent, and (b) positive
evidence of what the Chinese market does instead. Both halves are documented below.

### Negative evidence — searches run, nothing found

Nine targeted searches across the following returned **no** named-Chinese-voice-actor collab
headphone:
- Named top-tier CV talent: 边江 Bian Jiang, 阿杰 A Jie, 姜广涛 Jiang Guangtao, plus 夏磊, 杨天翔, 山新, 季冠霖
- 广播剧 (audio drama) + 联名 耳机 + 定制语音提示
- 声优 联名 耳机 + 中文配音 + 限量 + Tmall/JD
- VUP / 虚拟主播 + 联名 耳机 (A-SOUL, 泠鸢yousa)
- MissEvan store and 猫耳声刻 event merchandise

Everything confirmed on the Chinese side is game IP, anime IP, a **virtual singer voicebank**
(Luo Tianyi, Hatsune Miku), or a **Japanese** voice actress (Moondrop × 安野希世乃).
**Not one Chinese individual performer.** Recorded as an evidenced negative.

### Positive evidence — what China does instead

**2. Chinese CV talent economics — individual fees are modest; the content earns, not the person.**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|

| Successful creator monthly income | up to ~CNY 100,000 |
| 『魔道祖师』 audio drama earnings | **> CNY 10 million** |
| 『破云』, 『默读』 earnings | **> CNY 1 million each** |
| 『三体』 audio drama investment | ~CNY 10 million |
| Platform annual membership | CNY 138–148 |
| Per episode / season purchase | CNY 12.8 – 90+ |
| Tipping rate | 100 tips = CNY 10; one user tipped CNY 46,000 on a single title |

The article characterises audio drama as a 百亿 (hundred-billion-yuan) scale industry — **that is
an unsourced characterisation in the article itself, not a measured figure. Do not cite it.**

**Interpretation, stated as inference not fact:** at CNY 5,000–10,000 per episode, individual
Chinese CV talent has far less independent brand equity to license than a Japanese seiyuu with a
music label behind them (Kiramune) or a VTuber with an agency (774 inc.). The monetisable asset
is the *title*, not the *performer*. This is consistent with the absence of individual-talent
hardware collabs, but is not proof of causation.

## Consolidated JP vs CN structural comparison

| | Japan | China |
|---|---|---|
| Individual human talent collabs | **abundant** — Kiramune 13 seiyuu, Universal 6 seiyuu, Koiwai Kotori | **none found** |
| VTuber collabs | abundant — hololive, Nijisanji, 774 inc., Aogiri, indie long tail | **none found** |
| IP / game collabs | abundant | **abundant — the whole market** |
| Virtual singer / voicebank collabs | rare | **common** — Luo Tianyi, Hatsune Miku |
| Typical collab price | **JPY 5,980 – 22,880** (~USD 40–155) | **CNY 184 – 599** (~USD 26–83) |
| Production model | **made-to-order dominant**, zero inventory risk | standing e-commerce inventory, multi-channel price spread |
| Volume evidence | **25,000 units one wave** (ag × Patra); ¥166 mn crowdfund (KPro01) | **not disclosed anywhere**; CNY 7.71 mn audio-drama merch crowdfund contained no hardware |
| Talent-side hardware initiative | **yes** — Koiwai Kotori led development | **yes, but at platform level** — MissEvan launched its own earphones |
| Licensing visibility | **COVER discloses licensing revenue** | none disclosed by bilibili or any CN party |

FX note: USD figures above are **indicative only** — no FX rate has been fixed for this project
yet, and no USD figure anywhere in this dataset should be treated as final.
